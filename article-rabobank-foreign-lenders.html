Foreign lenders keep their day in court: Rabobank v Mwangi

The Court of Appeal holds that non-registration does not, by itself, bar a foreign lender from enforcing its loan in Kenya.

In Stichting Rabobank Foundation v Mwangi & another [2026] KECA 1550 (KLR), decided on 31 July 2026, the Court of Appeal held that a foreign lender's failure to register as a foreign company under section 974 of the Companies Act, 2015 does not, by itself, strip it of the capacity to sue and enforce its loan in Kenya.

The High Court had struck the claim out for want of standing. The Court of Appeal reversed, holding that whether the lender was carrying on business in Kenya is a disputed question of fact for trial, not a preliminary knock-out point.

For cross-border and development lenders, and the project-finance structures that lean on them, access to the courts is preserved. The decision does not settle the separate Central Bank licensing question, which the Business Laws (Amendment) Act, 2024 and the new credit-provider rules keep tightening. Structure and register with care.
